MHCflurry and NetMHCpan remain near random on the matched tissue-specific task, with AUROCs of 0.4874--0.5044 in the Human benchmark and 0.4917--0.5009 in the Mouse benchmark. Thus, general peptide--MHC scores do not explain the tissue-specific ranking. Removing tissue identity also creates conflicting labels for repeated peptide--MHC queries, and a trainable MHC-only control performs worse than TissuePMHC in every task (Supplementary Tables~\ref{S-tab:tissue-blind-conflict-audit} and~\ref{S-tab:trainable-tissue-blind-control}).

Tuned TissuePMHC reaches AUROC/AUPRC values of 0.8136/0.8126 in the Human benchmark and 0.8194/0.8279 in the Mouse benchmark. The strongest shared comparators reach 0.8033/0.8018 in Human (MLP dual-branch rank average) and 0.7753/0.7880 in Mouse (DB-MTL). Relative to untuned TissuePMHC, training-only tuning improves AUROC/AUPRC by 0.0054/0.0045 and 0.0489/0.0494, respectively. Task-wise effects are provided in Supplementary Figure~\ref{S-fig:tuning-delta-by-species}.

After matching MHC restriction, parent protein, and total positive tissue-occurrence count, sequence-based models retain above-random discrimination in both species. The occurrence-count-only score is exactly 0.5000 in every task, so the remaining signal is not explained by this controlled shortcut. It supports tissue-associated ranking in represented contexts, although residual study and detection structure may also contribute.
