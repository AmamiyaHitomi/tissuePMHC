Major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC-I) molecules display intracellular peptides to cytotoxic T cells, enabling immune surveillance and supporting vaccine development and cancer immunotherapy. Most existing predictors estimate general peptide presentation without considering tissue context. However, tissue-dependent antigen processing and peptide loading may cause different fragments of the same source protein to be preferentially presented across tissues, resulting in an underexplored dimension with potential value for tumor-antigen selection. We introduce TissuePMHC, a matched-pair benchmark and multi-task framework for learning tissue-specific preferences in MHC-I presentation. Across 77 human tissue--HLA tasks and 11 mouse tissue--H-2 tasks, TissuePMHC achieved mean AUROC/AUPRC values of 0.8136/0.8126 and 0.8194/0.8279, respectively. In contrast, the tissue-independent predictors MHCflurry and NetMHCpan produced near-random AUROCs of 0.4874--0.5044 in human and 0.4917--0.5009 in mouse, suggesting that general peptide--MHC scores do not capture the matched tissue-conditioned ranking. Together, these findings support tissue context as an informative dimension for relative peptide ranking in represented tissue--MHC combinations and provide parallel human and mouse benchmarks for further tissue-aware antigen-presentation research.
